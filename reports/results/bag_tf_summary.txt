llm-nav-shield: replay on costmaps accumulated through a real transform tree
============================================================================

The first bag replay (reports/results/bag_replay_summary.txt) used single
scans, because that dataset has no poses. This one has them.

Data (not committed; tools/bag_tf_scenarios.py derives everything from it):
  hallway_localization.bag, Magazino TORU, from Cartographer's public set
  https://storage.googleapis.com/cartographer-public-data/bags/toru/
  sha256 c9af90dadb9235f2755ed82bc56e1eadb70605982a52bcdd94ecd46c5a38052c
  40.4 MB, 137.7 s, 79024 messages, indoor warehouse hallway

  /odom       nav_msgs/Odometry     13771 msgs, 100 Hz
  /tf         tf2_msgs/TFMessage    27386 msgs, 199 Hz  (odom -> base_footprint)
  /tf_static  tf2_msgs/TFMessage    2 msgs
  /scan_front sensor_msgs/LaserScan 1748 msgs, 503 beams, 251 deg
  /scan_rear  sensor_msgs/LaserScan 1748 msgs, 475 beams, 237 deg

The transform chain is real and non-trivial:
  odom -> base_footprint -> base_link -> robot_center -> base_front_laser_link
                                                      -> base_rear_laser_link
with the lasers half a metre off centre at -45 and +135 degrees yaw. Each
scan is transformed through it at that scan's own pose, and 51 scans from
both lasers are accumulated per map, so the result carries real odometry
drift rather than one instantaneous view. The robot travels 82 m and its
odom pose runs from (-70.4, -28.2) to (-8.7, 26.6), so every map sits far
from the world origin and negative.

  scans per map      min 50, median 51, max 52
  unknown fraction   min 22.4%, median 44.5%, max 58.8%
  free edge cells    min 171, median 249, max 463 of 800

Transform timing
----------------
Scan header stamps trail their arrival by min 24.7, median 25.1, max 31.9
ms. --stamp-policy builds each map twice: once looking the pose up at the
scan's own stamp, once at the time the message was recorded, which is the
error a node makes when it uses "now" instead of the message stamp.

  grid cells differing   min 0.00%, median 2.64%, max 17.03%

Shield decisions changed by that error alone, same proposals, same goals,
only the map differs:

  straight-line proposals   1 of 40   scenario  9  halt -> recovery
  qwen proposals            2 of 39   scenario 11  recovery -> halt
                                      scenario 19  recovery -> halt

Roughly three to five percent of decisions flip, in both directions. The
halt-to-recovery case is the one that matters: a mis-timed map convinced
the shield a route existed where the correctly timed map says none does.
The recovery was still verified safe, but against a map that was wrong.

That is the limit of what this system guarantees, and it was not stated
anywhere before this run. The shield verifies a trajectory against the
costmap it is handed. Costmap correctness is a precondition of every
guarantee it makes, and transform timing is part of costmap correctness.

Result, qwen2.5:7b-instruct at temperature 0
--------------------------------------------
Same prompt template, same collect.py and parse.py. 39 of 40 responses
parsed to waypoint lists; the fortieth is a parse failure, kept as one.
Median 13.6 s per response on CPU.

  forwarded_safe          0
  recovered_from_unsafe   21
  recovered_from_off_goal 0
  halted_no_safe_path     18
  fallback_unsafe         0
  sealed-goal halt suite  10/10, 0 violations
  decision time           median 2.69 ms, max 4.20 ms

qwen produced no plan that was both safe and goal-reaching on any of the
39, against 1 of 40 on single-scan real maps and 2 of 40 on the synthetic
set. Accumulated maps are harder: 51 scans build walls that a single scan
leaves as unknown gaps, so 18 of 39 have no safe route at all.

Frame invariance
----------------
Every scenario replayed twice, window corner at the origin and at the
robot-centred origin, start, goal and waypoints shifted to match.

  0 bucket mismatches, with both straight-line and qwen proposals

Correction to the earlier bag result
------------------------------------
reports/results/bag_replay_summary.txt reports that with goals on the
window boundary the pre-fix shield produced 14 unsafe fallbacks out of 40
and the fixed shield none. On this dataset the same comparison produces
nothing: pre-fix and fixed both halt on all 40, zero fallback_unsafe,
zero buckets differing, with interior goals and with edge goals alike.

The accumulated hallway walls block edge goals outright, so no path is
found and the planner never gets the chance to route along the boundary.
On the museum-hall single-scan maps the goal was reachable that way, so
it did. The boundary bug is real and the fix is correct, but its firing
rate is a property of the environment, not a constant: 35% of one
dataset, 0% of another.

What this does not show
-----------------------
Odometry only. This bag's TF tree has no map frame and no localisation,
so drift accumulates unbounded and nothing here tests loop closure. The
robot is a warehouse AGV in a hallway, which is a narrower environment
than the synthetic rooms. Goal selection remains a heuristic, and the two
policies still disagree.

Reproduce
---------

      --out /tmp/tf_scen --count 40 --stamp-policy header

      --parsed reports/bag_tf/parsed/qwen2.5-7b-instruct.txt --out /tmp/tf_replay
